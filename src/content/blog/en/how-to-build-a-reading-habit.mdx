Most advice about reading more books assumes a discipline problem. Read every morning. Set a yearly goal. Try harder.

The real problem is simpler: your phone is engineered to win, and your book is not. So build the habit around that fact.

## Shrink the session

Do not commit to an hour. Commit to a session, ten focused minutes, page saved, timer running, phone face down after you start it. Small sessions stack. A reader doing four short sessions a week finishes far more books than a reader waiting for the perfect free Sunday.

## Make your library visible

You keep the habits you can see. A physical shelf works this way, and your digital one should too: covers that look like your actual books, a page number that remembers where you stopped, a shelf you are a little proud of. Rediscovering a book you already own beats buying another one you will not open.

## Put the book where the distractions live

The phone is not going away, so give the book a seat there. A widget with your current cover and page turns the home screen from a slot machine into a bookmark. One tap and you are back on the page, no feed between you and the book.

## Track rhythm, not guilt

A weekly rhythm, dots for the days you read, tells you the truth without punishing you. Miss a day and nothing breaks: your library is still there, unchanged. Guilt ends reading habits; rhythm sustains them.

## Close the app

This is the whole trick. Every tool above exists to get you off the screen and into the book faster. Start one ten-minute session tonight. The habit builds itself from there.
