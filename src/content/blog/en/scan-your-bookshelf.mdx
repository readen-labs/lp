Somewhere in your home there is a shelf of books you half remember owning. Cataloging them sounds like a weekend of data entry, which is why nobody does it.

With barcode scanning it is an afternoon, and a genuinely pleasant one.

## How the scan works

Every book printed in the last few decades carries an ISBN barcode on the back. Point your camera at it and Readen looks up the title, author, cover, and metadata, then drops the book onto your shelf. One book takes about a second. A full shelf takes minutes, not hours.

No barcode? Older editions can be added by searching the title. The point is momentum: shelf by shelf, pile by pile.

## Sort as you go

The magic is not the list, it is the honest sorting. As each book lands, give it a shelf: want to read, reading, or read. You will meet books you forgot you were excited about, and that pile becomes your reading queue for the next months. Free books, already paid for, already chosen by a past version of you.

## What a cataloged library changes

Once your books are in one calm place, three things happen. You stop buying duplicates. You start finishing what you own instead of browsing for what is next. And your library becomes something you can actually see, a record of the reader you have been, and a shelf for the reader you are becoming.

## An afternoon, honestly

Put on some music. Take the books down a shelf at a time. Scan, sort, put them back. When you are done, your entire reading life fits in your pocket, organized, offline, and yours.
