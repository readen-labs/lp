Search for a reading tracker app and you will find two kinds of products: tools that help you read, and feeds that use your books as content. The difference decides whether you finish more books or just log more time in an app.

Here is the checklist we would use, even if Readen did not exist.

## It should track books, not you

A good book tracking app has three honest shelves: want to read, reading, read. Your current page is always saved. That is the core, and it should work in seconds, scan a barcode, land on a shelf, done.

Be suspicious of apps where the first screen is other people's activity. Your library is not a timeline.

## Sessions beat streaks

Guilt is a terrible reading coach. Look for a tracker that measures reading in sessions, a timer, a page slider, a clean stop, rather than one that punishes you for missing a day. A streak should be a quiet record of rhythm, not a leash.

## Recommendations should have a name attached

Algorithmic suggestions optimize for engagement, which is why they drift toward whatever everyone is already reading. A curated catalog, where every book is there because a specific founder, scientist, or artist vouched for it, ages far better than a trending tab.

## The details tell you everything

Covers that look like books. Shelves that feel physical. An app that opens fast and closes without protest. Craft in the small things predicts respect in the big ones, especially with your data. No ads and no data selling should be table stakes.

## The one-question test

The best reading tracker is the one you close. If an app makes you want to put the phone down and open the book, it is doing its job. That is the standard we build Readen against, and the standard you should hold any app to, including ours.
